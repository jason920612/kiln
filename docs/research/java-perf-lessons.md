# java-perf-lessons: performance lessons from Paper/Folia/Moonrise/Minestom/Fabric optimization mods for a greenfield Rust Minecraft server core

## Scope and status (checked 2026-09-26)
Current reference points: Paper targets MC 26.3 (`mcVersion=26.3`). Folia is one version behind on `ver/26.2.x`. Moonrise is on `mc/26.3`, C2ME on `dev/26.3.0`, Leaf on `ver/26.2`, Purpur on `ver/26.3`. The open-source Pufferfish repo is stuck on `ver/1.21` (last push 2025-12-21). Starlight is discontinued; ScalableLux is its maintained fork. minecraft.wiki lists protocol 777 for 26.3. Mojang stopped obfuscating Java Edition from 26.1 onward, so vanilla source can now be read directly to check parity.

## What actually bottlenecks vanilla-like servers (evidence)
- **Entities dominate.** In the Meterstick benchmark (ICPE'23), entities took most tick time in every workload and produced 86.7-97.5% of network messages. Worst ticks reached 20.7x the mean; a TNT workload hit ticks over 2.5 s.
- **Single-thread ceiling is about 100 players.** Yardstick found Minecraft-like servers "poorly parallelized". Servo measured vanilla at about 110 players. Cubxity's baseline for Paper was about 100.
- **World generation is the other hard wall.**
  - Paper's chunk system rewrite: overworld went from 60.6 to 122.4 chunks/s with 16 workers.
  - C2ME (26.2 benchmark, heavy datapacks): 458 chunks/s at 16 threads, against Paper 142 and vanilla 42. At 96 threads it reached about 1381 against Paper's ~167.
  - Folia's own test gave 16 generation threads and generation was "still slow" at about 300 players.
- **Allocation is a constant tax.** The 1000-player Folia test allocated about 7.9 GB/s at peak. Lithium's `alloc.*` mixins and Leaf's many "remove stream" patches show the same thing.
- **Specific hot spots:**
  - Redstone dust: Alternate Current cuts its MSPT by up to 20x.
  - Hoppers: Lithium's event-driven design; Pufferfish claims 30% faster than Paper.
  - Villager `acquirePoi` and pathfinding.
  - Entity tracker: O(entities x players) in vanilla.
  - Voxel-shape merging, flagged as "super hot" in a Paper patch.
  - Lighting before 1.20: Starlight was 12-37x faster on updates.
  - DataFixerUpper world upgrades: Paper's DataConverter is 30-200x faster.

## Parallelism models tried in Java, and what they cost
**Folia (regionised multithreading).** This is the only model shown to scale with real traffic: 1000 players on a 16-core 7950X3D, about 630 at a steady 20 TPS after fixes, using 10-14 logical cores and about 2 Gbps outbound.

How it works, as read from the code:
- Chunks are grouped into sections of `2^gridExponent` chunks per side. The default is 4, so 16x16 chunks.
- Around each non-empty section it creates empty sections at radius 1 and looks for regions to merge with at radius 2, using Chebyshev distance.
- Recalculation needs at least 8 sections, and a split is considered once 1/6 of a region's sections are dead.
- Region states are transient, ready, ticking and dead. A ticking region never grows.
- A `StampedLock` protects region structure, and SWMR hash tables hold sections.
- Each region has its own tick deadline. The default scheduler is EDF by start time; work-stealing is the alternative.
- Per-region data (entities, block entities, block and fluid scheduled ticks, block events, connections, redstone time) merges and splits through callbacks. Redstone time is offset on merge.
- A global region keeps game rules, game time, daylight, console commands, world border and weather.

What it breaks:
- Every plugin, plus all scoreboard API.
- World load/unload and portal/respawn API.
- Synchronous `Entity#teleport`, which is removed for good in favour of `teleportAsync`.
- Block updates into chunks the region does not own are dropped, which Folia admits is explicit update suppression.
- Crowds don't split. Two loaded areas stay separate only when their non-empty sections are at least 4 sections apart, which is a gap of at least 48 chunks (derived from the code).
- The long tail is real: the Canvas fork carries "over 80 fixes" to region threading.

**Minestom.** Chunks are the partitions, assigned to threads round-robin, and the default is **1** dispatcher thread. Cross-thread `Acquirable` access takes one GLOBAL_LOCK and then the target thread's lock, so all cross-partition access is serialised. There are no vanilla features to begin with.

**Bolted-on async in Paper forks mostly failed:**
- Leaf dropped its async chunk sender in 26.2: "way too many thread-safety issues. After fixing them there's no real performance gain."
- Async target finding was removed in 1.21.8.
- Async pathfinding delivers results ticks later and is off by default.

**Parallel world ticking** (SparklyPaper, one thread per dimension) and **ShreddedPaper** (radius locks around a chunk) are niche.

## Techniques worth copying, and their parity cost
**Chunk system (Moonrise, now in Paper):**
- Ticket levels propagate in 64x64-chunk sections under area locks. The lock shift is the larger of the region shift and 6.
- Each generation status has a neighbour write radius (FEATURES 1, LIGHT 2) and a parallel-safe flag. EMPTY, STRUCTURE_STARTS, STRUCTURE_REFERENCES, BIOMES, TERRAIN and INITIALIZE_LIGHT are parallel-safe. FEATURES, LIGHT, SPAWN and FULL are not.
- Tasks that depend on an area go through a 16x16 `AreaDependentQueue`.
- Generation, loading, IO, compression and saving each have their own executor.
- Priority propagates to neighbouring chunks.
- Chunk, entity and POI data load separately, and unloading happens in two stages.
- Each player has ticket stages plus separate rate limiters for sending, loading and generating. Paper's defaults are 75 chunks/s sent and 100 chunks/s loaded.
- Vanilla parity is claimed.

**Lighting (Starlight):**
- BFS with separate increase and decrease queues on a FIFO.
- Nibble arrays stored per chunk section, not in a global hash map.
- Sky light initialised from a heightmap.
- `SWMRNibbleArray` keeps an updating copy and a visible copy, so other threads read light without locking.
- Mojang copied the core ideas in 1.20, so lighting is now well under 1% of generation time.
- Parity cost: light suppression is gone on Paper for good.

**Entities:**
- Each 16³ section keeps lists for all entities, hard-colliding entities, by class and by type.
- `NearbyPlayers` keeps per-chunk player lists at several radii.
- The tracker ticks off those per-chunk lists and only recomputes when something changes.
- A Paper PR that cut tracker updates on movement made move-packet handling up to 4x faster.

**Blocks:**
- Each section keeps a list of randomly-ticking blocks. Moonrise picks a random index in 0-4095 and ticks `list[idx]` if the index falls inside the list, which is statistically the same as vanilla without reading block states.
- A per-section flag marks "special colliding" blocks, meaning large collision shapes or moving pistons.
- Lithium adds specialised cuboid shapes and packed scheduled ticks.

**Block entities (Lithium):**
- Block entities can sleep until something wakes them.
- Inventories carry modification counters; neighbouring inventories are cached.
- Each entity section keeps change timestamps; failed transfers are skipped but their comparator updates are replayed.
- Includes a carefully handled 7-tick vs 8-tick hopper cooldown case.
- Parity is preserved. By contrast, Paper's `hopper cooldown-when-full` breaks dual-speed sorters (#13018).

**Redstone:** Alternate Current propagates power breadth-first and orders updates by flow direction, so update order is no longer locational. That is a parity trade-off.

**AI throttling:** Spigot's activation ranges (default 32/32/64/16/16/32/32) and Pufferfish DAB change gameplay:
- DAB: past 12 blocks, the brain ticks only every `clamp(d²>>8, 1, 20)` ticks.
- Villagers and several other mobs are blacklisted by default, and mobs can drown.
- Iron farms need redesigning under activation ranges.

**Paper's default exploit fixes:** TNT/carpet/rail duping, headless pistons, bedrock breaking, gravity-block duping through end portals, tripwire duping and zero-tick farms. Leaves exists to put these back.

**Network:**
- Paper ships Velocity natives: libdeflate for compression (about 2x zlib) and OpenSSL for AES-128-CFB8 (the protocol requires key = IV, continuous stream).
- Paper also adds flush consolidation, lock-free sending and packet bundles.
- Minestom writes a viewer's grouped packets once and fans the same bytes out to every viewer, and caches framed packets (`FramedPacket`/`CachedPacket`).
- Dyconits allows bounded inconsistency and cuts bandwidth by 49-85%.

**Storage:**
- Vanilla supports region compression `deflate|lz4|none` and defaults `sync-chunk-writes=true`.
- Paper saves at most 24 chunks per tick during autosave.
- The Linear format (whole region in one zstd stream) is about 50% smaller, but rewrites the whole region on every save. Paper refused zstd formats as "won't fix".

**Memory:** FerriteCore replaced per-state neighbour tables (about 600 MB in a big modpack) with a computed FastMap (about 7 MB), and deduplicated shapes.

## Takeaway for a Rust core
Build region ownership, the chunk-status graph, per-section metadata, SWMR lighting, section-bucketed entity indices, event-driven block entities, and serialise-once networking into the core data model from day one. Paper forks keep showing that these can't be retrofitted cheaply. Keep "vanilla-exact" as the default mode, with Paper-style throttles and fixes as opt-in settings.

## Design implications
- P0 — World ownership model. Make a Folia-style dynamic regioniser the core concurrency primitive from day one, even if v1 runs a single region. Region data lives in the region's task (Rust `&mut`, no `Arc<Mutex<World>>`). Define a `RegionData` trait with split-by-section and merge callbacks for entities, block entities, scheduled block and fluid ticks, block events, players and connections, redstone time, and the region's task queue. Put a global-region actor in charge of game rules, time, weather, world border, console, scoreboard and teams. Schedule each region against its own deadline (EDF by start time). Route cross-region operations through per-region inboxes that run on the target's next tick. Teleports and portals become remove, then async place. Start from Folia's defaults (16x16-chunk sections, Chebyshev create and merge radius 1) but make them tunable. Reject Minestom's per-chunk thread plus global lock, and reject one-thread-per-world as the main parallelism strategy.
- P0 — Design for hotspots that regions can't split. Crowds end up in one region, and Folia's gap rule means about 48+ chunks between loaded areas. Structure each region's tick into phases so read-only fan-out work can run on a thread pool against an immutable snapshot taken at phase start: entity-tracker visibility, pathfinding and POI candidate evaluation, packet encoding, light propagation for disjoint sections. Apply results deterministically in vanilla entity order within the same tick. Leaf's failed async bolt-ons show this has to be built into the data structures (versioned or copy-on-write sections, SWMR buffers), not added later.
- P0 — Chunk lifecycle state machine. Copy the Moonrise model:
- ticket-level propagation off the tick thread in 64x64-chunk sections under area locks;
- an explicit generation-status DAG (EMPTY, STRUCTURE_STARTS, STRUCTURE_REFERENCES, BIOMES, TERRAIN, FEATURES, INITIALIZE_LIGHT, LIGHT, SPAWN, FULL) with per-status neighbour read and write radii and parallel-safe flags;
- area-dependent scheduling for FEATURES and LIGHT, and priority that propagates to neighbours;
- separate chunk, entity and POI loads;
- two-phase unload with the explicit keep-alive checks;
- full-status transitions (inaccessible, border, block ticking, entity ticking) applied on the owning region's thread.
Never load a chunk synchronously on a tick thread: every API that might touch an unloaded or foreign chunk is async.
- P0 — Block storage layout. Use paletted containers that match the wire format (4-8 bit indirect, direct 15+ for blocks; 1-3 / 7+ for biomes; entries never cross longs) so chunk encoding is close to a memcpy plus header. Keep per-section metadata up to date: non-air count, fluid count, list of randomly-ticking blocks, special-colliding flag, and counters for blocks that listeners care about. Generate dense global block-state IDs at compile time, with arithmetic property transitions and shared collision shapes (the FerriteCore lesson).
- P1 — Lighting. Use Starlight's algorithm (FIFO BFS, separate increase and decrease queues, per-state opacity and conditional-shape flags, heightmap sky-light setup). Store nibbles per section inside the chunk, never in a global map. Make lighting a generation status with neighbour radius. Batch light updates per region at the end of each tick. Keep SWMR double-buffered visible arrays so chunk-send and save threads read without locking. Accept losing light suppression, or document it as a parity gap.
- P1 — Entity storage. Use generational entity IDs and per-16³-section spatial buckets with sub-indices by type/class plus a hard-colliding list. Keep per-chunk nearby-player lists at fixed radii (view distance, tick distance, 128-block spawn/despawn, small radii). Make tracker updates incremental: recompute only when a chunk's player set or the entity's chunk changes. Keep per-section change timestamps by entity category so hoppers and sensors can skip scans. ECS is optional; section-local, type-filtered queries are what matters, and entity ownership must move with the region.
- P1 — Event bus for block entities. Build inventory modification counters, change subscriptions, per-section block listeners and wake/sleep scheduling into the core so hoppers, furnaces, brewing stands and similar can sleep like Lithium's while matching vanilla timing (including the 7gt/8gt hopper cooldown case and comparator update patterns). Don't use Paper's `cooldown-when-full` style shortcuts in the default mode.
- P1 — Network pipeline.
- IO runtime separate from tick threads; each region hands outbound packets to connections through lock-free queues, flushed once per tick per connection.
- Broadcast packets are framed and compressed once and shared across N viewers (Minestom's FramedPacket plus per-viewer grouping with exclusion ranges); only encryption stays per connection (AES-128-CFB8 via the aes/cfb8 crates with AES-NI; decryption can be parallelised, encryption cannot).
- Compression via libdeflater or zlib-rs.
- Chunk packets built from section snapshots off the tick thread, with an optional per-chunk cache invalidated on modification; per-player anti-xray would bypass that cache.
- Per-player send, load and generate rate limiters (Paper defaults 75/100 chunks per second), client chunk-batch feedback, spiral ordering.
- Movement and entity metadata aggregation as a first-class bandwidth lever, with bounded-staleness (Dyconit-style) as an option.
- P1 — Save and IO pipeline. Dedicated IO and compression executors. Coalesce writes, and serve reads from pending writes. Incremental autosave with a per-tick budget. Configurable fsync (vanilla's `sync-chunk-writes=true` is expensive). Separate chunk, entity and POI region files for Anvil compatibility, plus an optional zstd region format. Upgrade old NBT with direct in-place map transforms in DataConverter's style, never DFU-style type machinery.
- P1 — World-generation throughput. Assume worldgen is the ceiling for exploration-heavy servers (Paper about 142 chunks/s at 16 threads, C2ME about 458). Parallelise the parallel-safe statuses across a work-stealing pool and lock FEATURES/LIGHT by area. Compile the density-function/noise-router tree into specialised code (as C2ME's DFC does), either with Rust code generation at load time or closure trees. Vanilla worldgen is already non-deterministic across runs (MC-55596), so exact matching of feature placement across chunk borders is neither required nor possible.
- P2 — Parity policy as config. Default to vanilla-exact: update order (DFS W,E,D,U,N,S with a 1,000,000 chain limit), hopper and redstone timing, dupes allowed, no activation range or DAB. Offer Paper-like fixes and throttles as opt-in settings: Alternate Current, activation ranges, DAB, hopper cooldown, dupe fixes, per-player spawns. Use the unobfuscated 26.1+ vanilla jars for differential tests.
- P2 — Observability and threads. Build per-region tick profiling (MSPT, time per phase), a watchdog and thread-context assertions in from the start; Canvas and Folia had to patch Spark to profile regions. Budget explicit thread pools (tick, worldgen, IO, network) with conservative defaults: Folia uses about cores/8 tick threads, Moonrise about cores/4 workers, and Folia keeps total threads under 80% of cores.

## Open questions
- How to scale crowded hotspots (for example 300+ players in one area) deterministically: which tick phases can safely fan out against snapshots while keeping vanilla entity-order semantics? There is no production Java precedent; Folia simply doesn't scale this case.
- Global mutable state in a regionised design: scoreboards, teams, advancements, global selectors in commands (`@e`), raids, the wandering-trader spawner, the dragon fight, map data. Folia still lists scoreboard API as broken, so a snapshot-plus-message protocol has to be designed from scratch.
- What does 'vanilla parity' mean here: bit-exact RNG streams (Moonrise already swaps the RNG), exact neighbour-update order, exact hopper timing? And should exploit fixes default on (Paper) or off (Leaves)?
- Tuning region section size for a Rust core: Folia's defaults (16x16 chunks, radius 1) need gaps of about 48 chunks or more; smaller sections split more often but merge and split more. This needs our own measurement.
- No public source gives a per-subsystem tick breakdown with numbers for vanilla at 100+ players (entity AI vs pathfinding vs tracker vs chunk ticking vs sending). A reproducible load harness (bot swarm plus world replay) is needed before choosing which micro-optimisations to prioritise.
- Plugin/extension model and its thread-safety contract. Folia shows that plugins written for one main thread break under regions. Do we expose region-scoped async APIs only (Folia-style schedulers) from v1?
- Chunk packet caching vs per-player anti-xray: can obfuscation be done as a cheap per-player patch on a shared encoded buffer?
- World format: Anvil-only for tool compatibility, or also an opt-in zstd/Linear-style format? Paper refused zstd formats; the reasons weren't visible.
- How much Minecraft 26.x data (for example the new TERRAIN status, the fluid count in chunk sections, protocol 777) changes over upcoming releases? Another research angle should confirm the exact protocol and data details.

## Omissions
- The only real-traffic evidence that regionised threading scales, the Folia 1000-player test, was run with players deliberately spread out. A plugin sent each new player to the least-occupied region across a pre-generated 100k x 100k world, in 49 teams of about 20, at view distance 8 and simulation distance 5. So there is no evidence that Folia-style regions help organic, clustered survival servers. That should lower the priority of 'regioniser as the P0 concurrency primitive' relative to intra-region phase parallelism for crowds, or at least be stated as an assumption.
- No stable Java reference exists for 26.3. Paper 26.3 is ALPHA, the stable line is 26.2, Folia 26.2 is BETA with 7 builds, and 26.4 snapshots with a new development protocol are already out. A Rust core should decide early between one fixed target version (26.2 stable vs 26.3) and a version-abstraction layer for protocol, registries and chunk-status graphs.
- 26.3 made breaking worldgen changes beyond the TERRAIN status: material_rule replaces surface_rule, noise_router is restructured into aquifers and ore_veins, chunk_surface_level is no longer interpolated, and carvers were reworked. Any plan to compile density functions (the C2ME DFC idea) or port vanilla worldgen must target the 26.3 data format, and Java optimisers may not have caught up yet.
- Most bandwidth bytes are not entity traffic (Meterstick: entities are 1.2-17.4% of bytes). Chunk encoding, caching, compression level and send-rate policy are therefore the main levers for bytes. Entity aggregation mainly cuts packet count and CPU. The P1 network plan should rank chunk-path work first for bandwidth.
- Offloading to a proxy is missing as a scaling axis. On Velocity-fronted networks, encryption ends at the proxy (backends run offline-mode with forwarding), and compression can be tuned per link. Hyperion, a Rust Minecraft event engine (hyperion-mc/hyperion, active 2026-09), pushes per-player work and 'regional multicasting' into horizontally scaled proxies. It reports 5000 bots at 1.42 ms tick on a 14-core laptop, though for custom events rather than vanilla survival. This contradicts the open question's 'no precedent' for crowded hotspots and suggests designing the packet fan-out boundary so it can move out of process.
- Platform caveat: the Java native accelerations the report cites (Velocity natives, epoll transport, Moonrise/Folia NUMA support via LinuxNuma) are Linux-only or Linux/macOS-arm64 only. The user develops on Windows. Performance baselines should be measured on the deployment OS (likely Linux), and the Rust stack should use cross-platform crates (aes/cfb8 with AES-NI, libdeflater, tokio).
- Folia's default tick-thread count is conservative, about cores/8, and the showcase test overrode it (12 region threads on 16 cores). Default thread budgets copied from Folia or Moonrise will under-use hardware unless they can be tuned.
- Several Java 'lessons' are already in upstream Paper as default patches: Moonrise, EntityScheduler optimisation (0016), Optimize Hoppers (0034), collision checks in move packets (0028), Anti-Xray (0025). Benchmarks against 'Paper' already include them, so a Rust core should be compared with current Paper, not vanilla, to claim wins.

- (medium, TS) Chunk sending is rate-limited per player and batched; chunk section encoding on the wire is close to the in-memory paletted layout. :: Paper defaults: `player-max-chunk-send-rate` 75 chunks/s, `player-max-chunk-load-rate` 100 chunks/s, `player-max-concurrent-chunk-generates` 0, `auto-config-send-distance` true. The protocol wraps chunks in Chunk Batch Start/Finished, and the client acknowledges with Chunk Batch Received. The current section format is: block count, fluid count, a block paletted container (0 bits = single value, 4-8 = indirect, 15+ = direct) and a biome paletted container (0 / 1-3 / 7+). Entries do not cross long boundaries (since 1.16), and since 1.21.5 the data array length is no longer sent. minecraft.wiki lists protocol 777 for 26.3. - (high, TS) Storage and IO: vanilla fsyncs chunk writes by default, Paper spreads autosave over ticks, whole-region zstd saves space but rewrites regions, and Paper's DataConverter replaced the slow DataFixerUpper. :: Vanilla: `region-file-compression` can be deflate, lz4 or none (default deflate, chunks not recompressed); `sync-chunk-writes` defaults to true. Paper: `max-auto-save-chunks-per-tick` 24 (incremental saving), `region-file-cache-size` 256, and it only writes chunks that serialise without throwing. Moonrise separates chunk, POI and entity region files and handles IO with priorities. Linear format (the whole 32x32 region as one zstd stream): about 50% smaller for the overworld and nether and about 95% smaller for the end (227 GB down to 119 GB in one real world), but each region must be held in memory and fully rewritten on save. Paper closed its zstd-format request as won't fix (#14045). DataConverter is at least 30x and up to 200x faster than DFU and mutates maps in place; in one real upgrade, 75% of the time was disk IO. - (high) FerriteCore shows that block-state memory needs dense IDs, computed state transitions and shared shapes. :: In large modpacks: vanilla's per-state `Table<Property, Value, State>` neighbour tables took about 600 MB and were replaced by one FastMap per block (an array indexed by arithmetic or bitmasks, about 7 MB total). ImmutableMap property storage took about 170 MB. Deduplicating blockstate shape caches saved about 200 MB. The ThreadingDetector per PalettedContainer cost 10-15 MB. These numbers come from modded clients, but the structural lesson holds: compute state transitions and share shapes instead of storing them per state.
